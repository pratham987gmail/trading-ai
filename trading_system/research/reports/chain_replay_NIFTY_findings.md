# Phase 1 edge check: NIFTY debit verticals, ₹20,000 micro-account

Generated by `research/backtest/chain_replay.py`, replaying
`analysis.signal_engine`'s candidate pipeline over `data/option_chains.db`
(the legacy recorder — 216,524 NIFTY chain snapshots across 357 trading
days, Sep 2024 – Sep 2026). Raw output: `chain_replay_NIFTY_baseline.json`,
`chain_replay_NIFTY_near_the_money.json`.

## Verdict: **no edge found. Do not build execution on this rule set as-is.**

Per the plan's Phase 1 gate (≥30 trades, net expectancy > 0, profit factor
≥ 1.3), the current rule-based selector fails outright:

| Run | Trades | Win rate | Net P&L | Profit factor | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (unmodified engine) | 76 closed | **0.0%** | **-₹8,960** | 0.0 | -44.8% of capital |
| Near-the-money filter (long leg ≤2% OTM) | 6 closed | 16.7% | -₹3,075 | 0.04 | -15.4% of capital |

Zero wins in 76 independent trades is not noise — a strategy with any real
edge, or even a coin-flip strategy, would show *some* winners across 76
tries. This is a structural problem, not bad luck.

## Root cause: the ₹2,000 risk budget cannot afford a real NIFTY vertical

NIFTY's lot size is 75. A vertical spread with strikes near spot (the kind
with actual directional meaning) costs roughly ₹2,000–2,500 in debit at
75x multiplier — already at or over the entire micro-account per-trade
budget, before considering that 2 open positions and a daily cap also have
to fit inside ₹20,000.

`analysis.risk_filters` correctly rejects every near-the-money candidate on
`RISK_MAX_LOSS_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`. What survives the budget gate is whatever is
cheap enough — which on this data meant strikes 1,000–1,400 points
out-of-the-money (4–5% from spot) with 3–7 days to expiry: debit as low as
₹0.05–₹1.10 per unit. These are not directional bets, they are lottery
tickets with a real-world probability of profit close to zero. The
₹94–138 round-trip cost per trade (fixed brokerage + STT + exchange + GST +
stamp + slippage) then exceeds the *entire value* of the position in most
cases — costs alone are why the baseline's average loss (-₹117.89/trade) is
close to its average round-trip cost (₹95.14/trade).

Forcing the selector toward the money (the second run) fixes the
"lottery ticket" problem but makes the budget problem worse: a real
near-the-money vertical fits the ₹2,000 cap on only 6 of 357 trading days
in this data. That sample is too small to trust on its own, but it is
still net negative, and 4 of the 6 losses were fast stop-outs (1–3 days),
suggesting the entry timing/direction call itself needs scrutiny, not just
the strike selection.

## What this does and does not test

- **NIFTY only.** SENSEX — the user's actual preferred underlying, lot 20,
  which would let a near-the-money vertical fit comfortably inside the
  ₹2,000 budget — has **zero recorded chains anywhere on this machine**.
  This replay cannot validate the SENSEX-first plan at all. Recording needs
  to start now (`scripts/record_chains.py` / `core/chain_recorder.py`) if
  BFO chain quoting works through SmartAPI (unverified — see the plan's
  "verify before coding" list); every day without a SENSEX recorder running
  is a day of chain history this analysis will never be able to use.
- **No bid/ask, anywhere, in this dataset.** A direct scan of all 20.8M
  recorded strike rows found 100% NULL on every bid/ask column — only
  LTP/OI/volume were ever captured by this legacy recorder
  (`recorder/storage.py` / `scripts/record_chains.py`, distinct from the
  newer `core/option_store.py` recorder, which has only ~400 snapshots
  total and is not usable for a study like this yet). Every fill in this
  replay is LTP plus a modelled slippage cost
  (`backtest.costs.CostModel.apply_slippage`), not a real crossable quote.
  Real slippage on strikes this thin, this far OTM, is very likely *worse*
  than the model assumes — if anything this replay is optimistic, not
  pessimistic.
- **Rule-based only.** No ML factor is involved (that's Phase 4, gated on
  this phase passing first, which it has not).
- **Costs are real.** `backtest.costs.CostModel` — the same model the
  backtester and (eventually) the live risk engine use — priced every
  entry and exit: brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, and
  slippage, all included in the P&L above.

## Recommendation

1. **Do not proceed to the execution engine (Phase 2/3) on this rule set.**
   The plan's own gate says so explicitly, and the data backs it.
2. **Start recording SENSEX chains now.** This is the single highest-value
   next action — it is pure data collection, blocks nothing else, and is
   the only way to test the account's actual preferred instrument. Verify
   BFO chain quoting works through SmartAPI first (untested assumption).
3. **If NIFTY is to be used at all on this capital, the strike-selection
   logic needs a moneyness/delta constraint that isn't in
   `analysis/spread_selector.py` today** — the risk-budget gate alone is not
   sufficient, because "cheap enough" and "has a real chance of profit" are
   not the same thing at this capital-to-lot-size ratio. That is a real
   code change to the shipped selector, not something to patch inside a
   replay script, and it should happen with the user's sign-off since it
   changes what the live system would actually select.
4. **The entry side (direction/timing) deserves its own look independent of
   strike selection** — the near-the-money run's fast stop-outs (1–3 days
   from entry) are a different failure mode than "the structure was never
   going to pay off," and conflating the two would misdiagnose a fix.
