# Does NIFTY pin to max pain on expiry day? No — and it's actively worse than doing nothing

Generated by `research/backtest/max_pain_pinning_study.py`, reusing
`analysis.options_analytics.max_pain()` unchanged (no formula
reimplemented) over all 75 real NIFTY expiry days recorded in
`data/option_chains.db`. Full output: `max_pain_pinning_NIFTY.json`.

## The claim being tested, and why the obvious test is misleading

Retail options commentary widely claims price "gravitates" to the
max-pain strike as expiry approaches — commonly cited as "NIFTY closes
within 200 points of max pain ~82% of the time on normal weeks." That
statistic, taken at face value, sounds like evidence of a real effect.
**It isn't, and testing it properly shows why**: on an index that
typically moves well under 200 points in an afternoon, *almost any*
reasonable estimate — including simply the current price, which involves
no options analysis at all — will also land within 200 points of the
close. A number being "close to the close" is not evidence it is pulling
price toward itself; it may just be close because nothing moved much
that day.

**The only way to tell a real pinning force from that coincidence:**
compare max pain's distance from the actual close against **spot's own**
distance from the actual close, measured at the same moment. If max pain
isn't a *better* predictor than "just use the current price," there is no
effect to trade.

## Result: max pain loses to the naive baseline, at every single checkpoint

| Time of day | Mean max-pain error (pts) | Mean spot error (pts) | Max pain closer than spot | Edge (spot error − max-pain error) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11:00 | 175.3 | 94.7 | 37.3% | **-80.6** |
| 12:00 | 164.4 | 80.3 | 37.3% | **-84.1** |
| 13:00 | 154.5 | 68.7 | 38.7% | **-85.8** |
| 14:00 | 133.4 | 50.4 | 42.7% | **-83.0** |
| 14:30 | 122.2 | 47.8 | 53.3% | **-74.4** |
| 15:00 | 111.3 | 33.3 | 36.0% | **-77.9** |
| 15:15 | 105.4 | 22.6 | 34.7% | **-82.9** |

(n = 75 real expiry days at every checkpoint — no thin-data days were
skipped.)

The edge column is negative at every checkpoint, by 74–86 points,
throughout the day. Current spot price is a consistently, substantially
better predictor of the eventual close than max pain is — including
right up to the last pre-close snapshot (15:15), where spot's own error
(22.6 points) is less than a quarter of max pain's (105.4 points). Max
pain "closer than spot" never exceeds 53.3% — worse than a coin flip at
every checkpoint but one, and that one (14:30) is still under 55%.

**Verdict: `NO_PINNING_EDGE_FOUND`.** There is no evidence in this data
that price is pulled toward max pain. Max pain does shrink in absolute
terms as the day progresses (175 → 105 points), but so does spot's own
error, and spot's shrinks faster and further — the pattern is fully
explained by "prices don't move much in the last hour," not by any
gravitational pull toward the writer-optimal strike.

## What this does and does not rule out

- **Rules out:** a naive "trade toward max pain" strategy on NIFTY,
  using the standard max-pain formula, at any point during expiry day
  tested here.
- **Does not rule out:** max pain having predictive value during
  genuinely quiet, event-free weeks specifically (this study pooled all
  75 expiry days together, event weeks included) — the sources
  themselves flagged max pain as "unreliable during event weeks." A
  follow-up could stratify by a realized-volatility filter, but given
  how uniformly negative this result is across every checkpoint, that
  refinement looks unlikely to flip the sign, only possibly narrow the
  gap somewhat.
- **Does not test SENSEX** — no recorded chain history exists yet.
