# Phase 1 edge check: NIFTY debit verticals, ₹20,000 micro-account

Generated by `research/backtest/chain_replay.py`, replaying
`analysis.signal_engine`'s candidate pipeline over `data/option_chains.db`
(the legacy recorder — 216,524 NIFTY chain snapshots across 357 trading
days, Sep 2024 – Sep 2026). Raw output: `chain_replay_NIFTY_baseline.json`,
`chain_replay_NIFTY_near_the_money.json`, `chain_replay_NIFTY_wide_stop.json`.

**Update:** the "near-the-money" run below uses the real, shipped
`RISK_TOO_FAR_FROM_MONEY` gate (`analysis.risk_filters`, wired through
`Settings.SIGNAL_MAX_MONEYNESS_PCT`), added directly to
`analysis/spread_selector.py`'s candidate pipeline as a result of this
finding — not a script-local workaround. It is off by default
(`SIGNAL_MAX_MONEYNESS_PCT=None`), so the unmodified engine's baseline
numbers below are unaffected.

## Verdict: **no edge found either way. Do not build execution on this rule set as-is.**

Per the plan's Phase 1 gate (≥30 trades, net expectancy > 0, profit factor
≥ 1.3), the current rule-based selector fails outright under both configurations:

| Run | Trades | Win rate | Net P&L | Profit factor | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (moneyness gate off) | 76 closed | **0.0%** | **-₹8,960** | 0.0 | -44.8% of capital |
| Moneyness gate on (long leg ≤2% OTM) | 85 closed | 16.5% | **-₹39,278** | 0.32 | -185.5% of capital |

Two different, equally clear failure modes:

- **Gate off:** the risk budget forces far-OTM near-worthless structures.
  Zero wins in 76 trades is not noise — any real edge, or even a coin-flip
  strategy, would show *some* winners across 76 tries.
- **Gate on:** real near-the-money spreads have real premium at risk, so
  when the directional call is wrong the losses are large and real, not
  the tiny far-OTM losses from the baseline. Net loss is over 4x worse in
  rupee terms even though the trade count and win rate both improved. This
  points at the entry/direction logic (`analysis.technical`'s trend
  classification feeding the BULLISH/BEARISH call), not just strike
  selection, as broken or at least unvalidated at this timeframe — see
  "Root cause 2" below, which now has a full diagnosis, not just a hunch.

**Caveat on the -185.5% max drawdown:** this replay does not model account
ruin. It keeps opening new trades against the original ₹20,000 regardless
of cumulative equity, so a drawdown past 100% means a real account would
have been margin-called or simply out of capital long before trade 85. The
rupee and percentage P&L figures are a real, cost-inclusive tally of what
each trade would have made or lost in isolation; they are not a compounding
equity curve, and nothing here should be read as "the account survived this
whole period."

## Root cause 1 (fixed): the ₹2,000 risk budget forced far-OTM structures

NIFTY's lot size is 75. A vertical spread with strikes near spot costs
roughly ₹2,000–2,500 in debit at 75x multiplier — at or over the entire
micro-account per-trade budget. `analysis.risk_filters` correctly rejected
every near-the-money candidate on `RISK_MAX_LOSS_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, and what
survived was whatever was cheap enough: strikes 1,000–1,400 points
out-of-the-money (4–5% from spot), debit as low as ₹0.05–₹1.10 per unit —
lottery tickets, not directional bets, with round-trip costs (₹94–138)
that exceeded the entire position value in most cases.

**Fix shipped:** `RiskLimits.max_moneyness_pct` /
`Settings.SIGNAL_MAX_MONEYNESS_PCT` (`analysis/risk_filters.py`,
`config/settings.py`) — a new `RISK_TOO_FAR_FROM_MONEY` gate, evaluated per
candidate alongside every other risk gate, off by default. Setting it
forces the selector to only consider candidates within that band of spot,
regardless of how attractively the rupee budget lets a far-OTM structure
look on paper.

## Root cause 2 (diagnosed, partially mitigated, not fixed): two separate problems, disentangled

`ReplayTrade` now records `entry_spot`/`exit_spot` per trade and derives
`spot_moved_correctly` (did the underlying move the direction this trade
needed, independent of whether costs/theta still made it a loss). Cross-
tabulating that against `exit_reason` on the 85-trade moneyness-gate run
separates two distinct failures that were previously conflated:

| Exit reason | Spot moved correctly | Count | Wins | Net P&L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STOP | No | 57 | 0 | **-₹50,810** |
| STOP | Yes | 10 | 0 | **-₹5,836** |
| TIME_DTE | Yes | 10 | 6 | +₹3,009 |
| TARGET | Yes | 8 | 8 | +₹14,359 |

Two findings, not one:

**2a. The direction call is worse than a coin flip.** Only 32.9% of trades
had spot move the intended direction by exit (`direction_correct_pct`).
`TARGET` and `TIME_DTE` exits are 100%/60% win rate respectively and both
only ever occur when the call was right — the entire net loss is
concentrated in the 67 `STOP` exits. This is `analysis.technical`'s trend
classification (feeding the BULLISH/BEARISH choice) failing to predict
NIFTY's move over a 3–7 day horizon on this data, or the entry timing
(first snapshot at/after 10:00 IST) catching moves that are already
exhausted. Not iterated on in this pass.

**2b. The 50%-of-debit stop is too tight, independent of (2a).** 10 of the
67 stop-outs happened despite spot moving *correctly* — the position was
right and still got killed. 18 of 67 stops fired with the underlying
having moved less than 0.3% (essentially flat), meaning theta decay and
day-to-day noise in a thin near-the-money spread's value can trip a
50%-of-debit stop with no real adverse move at all.

**Tested directly:** re-running the full dataset with the stop loosened
from 50% to 80% of debit (`stop_fraction_of_debit=0.8`, same moneyness
gate, `chain_replay_NIFTY_wide_stop.json`) — a real, measured experiment,
not a guess:

| Stop | Trades | Win rate | Direction correct | Net P&L | Profit factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% (original) | 85 | 16.5% | 32.9% | -₹39,278 | 0.32 |
| 80% (wider) | 75 | 22.7% | 42.7% | -₹27,230 | 0.51 |

Loosening the stop alone recovered about 30% of the net loss and nearly
doubled the profit factor — confirming 2b is real — but the system is
**still a clear loser** (profit factor 0.51, still under the 1.3 gate).
Direction correctness also rose with the wider stop (32.9% → 42.7%),
which makes sense: fewer positions are killed by noise before the
underlying's actual move (right or wrong) has time to register. It also
means **2a is still the dominant, unsolved problem** — even a "perfect"
stop cannot fix a direction call that is wrong more often than not.

## What this does and does not test

- **NIFTY only, still.** SENSEX — the account's actual preferred
  underlying, lot 20, where a near-the-money vertical would fit the ₹2,000
  budget comfortably — had zero recorded chains when this analysis was
  first run. **This is now being corrected**: a continuous SENSEX/BFO chain
  recorder (`scripts/record_chains.py --symbols SENSEX --exchange BFO`) was
  started and confirmed working (100% completeness on the first capture,
  verifying SmartAPI does support BFO chain quoting — previously an open
  question). It writes to `data/options.db` via the current
  `core.option_store.OptionChainStore` schema. This replay script
  (`chain_replay.py`) targets the older `recorder.storage.ChainStorage`
  schema and will need a small adapter, or a new replay entry point, once
  enough SENSEX history has accumulated to be worth analysing — there is
  no shortcut to accumulating that history; it only comes from the recorder
  running through real trading sessions.
- **No bid/ask, anywhere, in the legacy NIFTY dataset.** A direct scan of
  all 20.8M recorded strike rows found 100% NULL on every bid/ask column —
  only LTP/OI/volume were ever captured by this legacy recorder. Every fill
  in this replay is LTP plus a modelled slippage cost
  (`backtest.costs.CostModel.apply_slippage`), not a real crossable quote.
  Real slippage on strikes this thin is very likely *worse* than the model
  assumes — if anything this replay is optimistic, not pessimistic. (The
  newer recorder now running for SENSEX captures full depth via the
  `FULL` SmartAPI quote mode, so this limitation should not recur once
  there is enough SENSEX history to replay.)
- **Rule-based only.** No ML factor is involved (that's Phase 4, gated on
  this phase passing first, which it has not).
- **Costs are real.** `backtest.costs.CostModel` — the same model the
  backtester and (eventually) the live risk engine use — priced every
  entry and exit: brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, and
  slippage, all included in the P&L above.

## Recommendation

1. **Do not proceed to the execution engine (Phase 2/3) on this rule set.**
   The plan's own gate says so explicitly, and the data backs it under all
   three configurations tested (baseline, moneyness gate, moneyness gate +
   wider stop).
2. **SENSEX recording is now running.** No further action needed to start
   it; it needs time (real trading sessions) to accumulate a usable sample.
3. **Widen the stop as a standing change** — `STOP_FRACTION_OF_DEBIT` in
   `chain_replay.py` (and, once a real execution engine exists, the
   equivalent live exit rule) from 0.5 toward something nearer 0.8. This is
   a measured improvement, not a hunch, but it is not sufficient on its own.
4. **The direction call (2a) needs its own investigation** — this is the
   dominant unsolved problem and real analysis work, not a settings change.
   Concretely: test whether `analysis.technical`'s trend classification has
   any forward predictive value at this holding period at all (a
   feature-vs-forward-return correlation study, not another backtest), and
   whether the fixed 10:00 IST entry check is systematically late.
5. **Re-run this replay whenever the direction logic changes**, and once
   SENSEX history exists, using the same gates so results stay comparable
   to this baseline.
